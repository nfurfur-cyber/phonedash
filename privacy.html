폰대시(PhoneDash) 안드로이드 앱과 Windows PC 프로그램에 적용됩니다.

한눈에 보기

  • 동의 없이는 수집하지 않습니다. 폰대시에는 계정, 자체 서버, 광고, 오류 보고가 없습니다. 동의하지 않으면 개발자는 여러분의 데이터를 받지 않습니다.
  • 이용 통계(선택): 폰 앱에서 "통계 보내기"를 누른 경우에만 Google 애널리틱스(Firebase)로 보냅니다. 연결 방식(USB·Wi-Fi), 페이지·버튼·위젯 개수와 위젯 종류, 테마·언어, Pro 여부, Pro 화면을 열었는지 같은 이용 단계와 애널리틱스가 자동으로 기록하는 정보(첫 실행, 사용 시간, 기종·Android 버전, IP로 추정한 국가·지역, 설치 경로, 인앱 구매, 설치마다 새로 만드는 무작위 ID)입니다. 목적은 앱 개선이며, 페이지 이름·입력한 글·PC 정보·광고 ID는 보내지 않습니다. 전체 페이지 화면의 "이용 통계: 켬/끔" 버튼으로 언제든 끌 수 있고, 끄면 통계용 ID가 초기화되고 아직 보내지 않은 통계는 버립니다. 이미 보낸 통계는 데이터 삭제의 방법으로 지울 수 있습니다.
  • 별점 요청: 일정 기간 쓴 뒤 Google Play의 평가 창이 뜰 수 있습니다. 평가 내용은 Google Play로만 가고, 앱은 평가했는지 알 수 없습니다.
  • PC와 폰 사이에서만 오갑니다. 화면에 보이는 PC 정보(CPU·GPU·메모리·재생 중인 음악·AI 사용량 등)는 PC에서 읽어 USB 케이블(또는 직접 연결한 Wi-Fi, 고정 인증서와 키로 암호화)로 여러분의 폰에만 보냅니다.
  • 기능에 필요한 요청만 밖으로 나갑니다. 날씨(open-meteo.com: 고른 도시의 좌표), 프로그램 업데이트 확인(GitHub), adb가 없을 때 adb 내려받기(Google) 같은 요청입니다. 각 서비스는 일반 웹 요청처럼 IP 주소를 봅니다. 전체 목록과 끄는 방법은 아래 영문 상세 목록에 있습니다.
  • 마이크(RECORD_AUDIO)는 받아쓰기 위젯의 마이크 버튼을 눌렀을 때만, 처음 한 번 권한을 묻습니다. 음성은 폰의 음성 인식 서비스(대부분 Google)가 처리하고, 인식된 글자만 여러분의 PC로 보내 입력합니다.
  • 결제는 Google Play가 처리합니다. 개발자는 Google이 모든 개발자에게 주는 주문 정보만 받습니다(카드 정보 없음).
  • 저장 위치: 설정은 PC의 %APPDATA%\PhoneDash, 기록은 %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhoneDash, 폰 앱의 캐시는 앱 저장공간에만 있습니다. 앱이나 PC 프로그램을 지우면 함께 지울 수 있습니다.
  • 문의: Google Play의 폰대시 페이지에 있는 개발자 이메일로 연락해 주세요.

데이터 삭제

폰대시(PhoneDash)에는 계정이 없어서 지울 계정 정보도 없습니다. 데이터는 이렇게 지웁니다.

  • 폰: 앱을 삭제하거나 Android 설정 › 앱 › 폰대시 › 저장공간 › 데이터 삭제를 누르면 앱이 폰에 저장한 것이 모두 지워집니다.
  • PC: PC 프로그램을 제거한 뒤 %APPDATA%\PhoneDash와 %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhoneDash 폴더를 지우세요.
  • 이용 통계: 전체 페이지 화면에서 이용 통계: 끔으로 바꾸면 더 보내지 않고, 통계용 ID를 초기화하고, 아직 보내지 않은 통계를 버립니다. 이미 보낸 통계는 Google Play의 폰대시 페이지에 있는 개발자 이메일로 삭제를 요청해 주세요. 통계에는 이름이나 이메일이 없어 이메일 주소로는 찾을 수 없으니, 기종·국가·통계를 켠 시기를 함께 적어 주세요. 그 정보로 특정할 수 있는 기록을 지웁니다.
  • 보관 기간: 설치 ID가 붙은 통계는 요청이 없어도 마지막 이용 후 최대 14개월이 지나면 Google 애널리틱스에서 자동으로 지워집니다. ID가 없는 합계는 남습니다.
  • 구매 기록은 Google Play가 보관하며 Google 계정에서 관리합니다.

Details (English)

PhoneDash is based on the open-source PhoneBox (MIT licence). PhoneDash has no accounts, no servers of its own, no ads and no crash reporting. The phone app can send usage statistics to the developer through Google Analytics, only if you agree (see Usage statistics). Without your yes, and from the PC program in any case, the developer receives no data from you. This page lists every place the agent (on your computer) and the Android app (on your phone) connect to, so you can see exactly what leaves your machine and turn it off.

Last updated: 2026-11-25 (PhoneDash 1.0.0).

Purchase. The phone app is free; PhoneDash Pro is sold in it through Google Play. Google handles the payment; the developer receives only the order information Google provides to every developer (no card details).

Usage statistics

Off until you tap Send statistics in the question the phone app shows after it has been connected for a while. Statistics: on / off in the page overview changes the answer at any time; turning it off also resets the statistics ID and drops what had not been sent yet (statistics already sent stay with Google Analytics until they are deleted or expire). It uses Google Analytics for Firebase (Google), and the developer uses it only to see what to improve and sees totals, not names.

Sent while it is on, from the phone app only:

  • How the panel is set up, once a day: USB or Wi-Fi, how many pages, buttons and widgets there are, the theme's and language's code, and whether Pro is owned; the kinds of widgets in use (such as "clock"), never their contents.
  • Steps taken: opening the page overview, seeing a locked page, opening the Pro sheet and tapping Buy, and that Google Play was asked for its rating card.
  • What Google Analytics records by itself: first start, sessions and time of use, app updates and removal, in-app purchases (product and price), the phone's model, Android version, language and app version, the country and region worked out from the IP address, where the app was installed from, and a random ID for this installation (not for you; reinstalling makes a new one).

Never sent: page or button names, anything typed or dictated, computer names or readings, what plays, AI sessions, addresses, or the advertising ID (the app does not have the permission to read it). Ad features are off. A copy of the app built without the developer's Firebase project has no statistics at all. See Deleting your data for statistics already sent.

Deleting your data

PhoneDash has no accounts, so there is no account to delete. What there is goes away like this:

  • On the phone: uninstall the app, or use Android's Settings › Apps › PhoneDash › Storage › Clear data.
  • On the computer: uninstall the program, then delete the folders named under What stays on your devices.
  • Usage statistics: Statistics: off in the page overview stops them, resets the statistics ID and drops what had not been sent yet. To have statistics already sent deleted, write to the developer e-mail address on the PhoneDash Google Play page. They hold no name or e-mail address to look you up by, so say which phone model and country it was and roughly when statistics were on; what can be told apart by that is deleted.
  • How long they are kept: without a request, statistics tied to the installation's ID are deleted by Google Analytics at the latest 14 months after the last use. Totals without an ID remain.
  • Purchases are kept by Google Play and managed in your Google account.

What stays on your devices

  • Settings and layouts: config.json in %APPDATA%\PhoneDash (Windows), ~/.config/PhoneDash (Linux) or ~/Library/Application Support/PhoneDash (macOS), plus data.json (pushed values and notifications) next to it. On macOS and Linux these files are readable only by your user (0600).
  • Discord login: if you use the Discord widget, discord-token.json next to config.json holds the access and refresh token Discord gave PhoneDash (on macOS and Linux readable only by your user).
  • Steam key: the SteamID64 and Web API key are stored in config.json on this computer. A custom profile name is sent to Steam once when you save it, then only the SteamID64 is stored. The key is never sent to the phone or logged. The activity widget derives its short history from successive polls in memory; it is not written to disk or logged and starts empty after an agent restart.
  • Last Claude quota reading: claude-quota.json next to the log (usage percentages and reset times, no token), so the panel can show the last values after a restart, and claude-quota-schedule.json (when the next check may run, the last error message and, after Anthropic refused a token, a 16-character hash of that token, never the token itself), so a restart doesn't ask Anthropic again sooner than it should.
  • Log: agent.log in %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhoneDash (Windows), ~/.local/share/PhoneDash (Linux) or ~/Library/Application Support/PhoneDash (macOS).
  • Phone app: caches (weather, rates, widget state) in its own app storage. The app asks for INTERNET, VIBRATE, WAKE_LOCK and the Play Store's install referrer (both added by the statistics library, unused while statistics are off) and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (only to tell which network is Wi-Fi when the Wake button sends its packet; Android grants it without a prompt), and for the microphone (RECORD_AUDIO) only when a Dictation widget's button is tapped for the first time.
  • Computer data shown on the panel (CPU, GPU, memory, disks, processes, media, volume, AI coding sessions and quotas, Docker / Podman, OBS) is read locally and goes only to your phone: over the USB cable (adb reverse to 127.0.0.1, with a key only the PhoneDash app has) or, after pairing, over Wi-Fi / Tailscale with a pinned certificate and a key. On Linux, an Intel GPU's load comes from the GPU usage counters in other processes' /proc/<pid>/fdinfo (the counters only, nothing those programs draw or compute); processes of other users are read only when Linux allows the agent to read them (normally as root or with CAP_SYS_PTRACE).
  • Claude Code conversation (off by default): with Settings › Claude Code › "Show the conversation on the phone" on, tapping a Claude Code session in the sessions widget asks the agent for its last 30 messages (your prompts and Claude's replies, each cut at 4000 characters) and its last 12 steps. The agent reads them from that chat's file in ~/.claude/projects and sends them only to your phone, only for a session that is running, and only while the details are open. A step is the tool name plus a file name, the program a command starts with (never the command line) or a web host; tool output, thinking and file contents are never sent. Nothing is stored on the phone or the computer. With the switch off the agent refuses the request and no prompt or reply text is sent.
  • Cursor sessions (off by default; Settings › General › Cursor sessions): while enabled, the agent scans recent *.jsonl files under ~/.cursor/projects/*/agent-transcripts (about every 30 seconds for discovery) and reads a bounded tail of each file to parse the last record's role in memory. Only that role and the file times are kept for the panel; chat text is never shown, stored or sent. Project labels use the folder basename from Cursor's workspaceStorage/*/workspace.json folder URI (cached about a minute), sent to the phone as the session's project name over the same local USB / paired Wi-Fi link as other panel data. Turning the setting off stops the scan.
  • Antigravity sessions (off by default; Settings › General › Antigravity sessions): while enabled, the agent scans recent transcript.jsonl files under ~/.gemini/antigravity/brain and ~/.gemini/antigravity-cli/brain (about every 30 seconds for discovery) together with local annotations/*.pbtxt metadata and the Antigravity CLI's history.jsonl workspace basename. Only step types, statuses, timestamps, model names, tool names and token counters are kept; prompts, replies, thinking and tool arguments are never shown, stored or sent. Titles reach the phone only while Show Antigravity chat titles on the phone is also on; otherwise the widget shows the project name or Antigravity. Turning the setting off stops the scan.
  • OpenCode sessions (off by default): with Settings › General › "Show OpenCode sessions" on, the agent asks OpenCode's own background service — the native one, and the one inside each running WSL distro — for its recent sessions: the same local HTTP API the desktop app uses, authenticated with the password OpenCode wrote to ~/.local/state/opencode/service.json; that password never leaves the computer. Only session metadata is requested (the folder, title, model, token counters, start and update times and the outcome); prompts, replies and tool arguments are never fetched, and sessions older than three hours are ignored. A stopped WSL distro is never started for this. Titles reach the phone only while Settings › General › "Show OpenCode session titles" is also on; otherwise the widget shows the project folder. The data goes only to your phone over the same local USB / paired Wi-Fi link as other panel data.
  • OpenCode permission prompts and questions (off by default): when OpenCode's background service asks for permission, the agent reads the pending request from its local API (127.0.0.1, with the password OpenCode wrote to ~/.local/state/opencode/service.json in a Basic header, over loopback only — it is never sent anywhere else). What goes to the phone is the card's content only: the project, the action (run a command, edit a file…), the command or paths it names, any message OpenCode attached, and the pattern an Allow always would save for the project. The phone's answer (allow once, allow always, deny) is posted back to the same local API. The same card carries OpenCode's agent questions: the question, its choices and whether a typed answer is allowed go to the phone, and the pick or typed answer comes back — nothing else about the conversation is read. Nothing is offered or sent until Settings › General is turned on (or "phoneApprovals": true in the opencode section of config.json); turning it off again stops this entirely.
  • Claude Code chat titles (off by default): with Settings › Claude Code › "Show Claude Code chat titles on the phone" on, the sessions widget shows each chat's title, the name you gave it or the one Claude Code wrote, which it often draws from your first prompt. The agent reads only the title records at the end of the chat's file in ~/.claude/projects, never the prompts, replies or tool arguments, and sends the title only to your phone, like the rest of this list. With the switch off the file is not opened and no title is sent.
  • Sound for the Spectrum bars widget (Windows): while an enabled page with the widget is on the phone's screen, the agent captures what the default output device plays, analyzes it in memory and sends only 48 loudness levels per frame to the phone. Audio is never stored or sent; capture stops a few seconds after the widget leaves the screen, and removing the widget or turning its page off turns it off.
  • Mouse control for the Trackpad widget: touches and scroll gestures are sent directly from the phone to the agent on your computer (over USB or pinned-TLS Wi-Fi) to control the cursor and scroll. No mouse or gesture data is ever recorded, stored or sent outside your local connection. It is active only while an enabled page contains the Trackpad widget with Allow controlling the mouse from the phone enabled (off by default). On a Mac, a three-, four- or five-finger gesture from the phone opens Mission Control or Apps, signals the Dock, or presses a key (moving between desktops or pages). While you gesture, the agent reads the Mac's Trackpad, com.apple.dock and com.apple.symbolichotkeys preferences (at most once every 2 seconds) to do what your own trackpad would do. Only the gesture settings are kept, in memory, and nothing is stored or sent.
  • Local services: LibreHardwareMonitor's web server (127.0.0.1:8085), a running Antigravity language server (loopback, over HTTP or HTTPS), the Docker / Podman socket, OBS WebSocket and the Discord desktop app's local connection (a named pipe or socket on this computer) are local connections.

scripts/uninstall.ps1 -Purge and scripts/uninstall.sh --purge remove the agent together with its settings, pushed data and log (without the switch, settings are kept). Uninstalling the Android app removes everything on the phone.
